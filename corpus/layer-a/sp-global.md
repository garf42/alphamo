
# S&P Global Ratings

## Research Summary

Verified primary facts as of May 2026: S&P Global Ratings traces to Henry Varnum Poor 1860 publication + Standard Statistics Bureau 1906; merged 1941 into Standard & Poor's; acquired by McGraw-Hill 1966; reorganized as S&P Global 2016 (from McGraw Hill Financial). February 2022 completed $44B all-stock merger with IHS Markit. Current S&P Global has four reportable segments: Ratings, Indices, Market Intelligence, Commodity Insights (Platts), plus Mobility (auto/CARFAX). FY2025: revenue $15.336B (+8%), GAAP net income $4.471B (+16%), diluted EPS $14.66 (+19%). Ratings +12%, Indices +14%, Market Intelligence +7%. 2025 Billed Issuance +28% supporting Ratings transaction revenue. FY2026 guidance: organic constant currency revenue growth 6.0-8.0%, diluted EPS $19.40-$19.65. Market cap ~$170B (2026).

Critical force-topology updates training data would miss: (1) 2022 IHS Markit merger fundamentally restructured the architecture from a ratings-and-S&P-500-focused entity into a four-segment information services conglomerate; merger was largest in S&P Global history. (2) Ratings + Indices segments together drove >70% of operating profit despite being <50% of revenue — duopoly-protected ratings (with Moody's) and S&P 500 index licensing remain the load-bearing accumulated forces. (3) 2008 financial crisis settlement — $1.375B DOJ + states settlement Feb 2015 (vs Moody's $864M Jan 2017); both architecturally survived with NRSRO regulatory protection preserved. (4) Passive investing tailwind — SPIVA mid-2025 shows 91% of large-cap active managers underperformed S&P 500 over 20 years; passive AUM growth driving S&P 500 + Dow Jones index licensing revenue growth at +14% YoY 2025. (5) With Intelligence acquisition November 2025 + ongoing tuck-ins extending Market Intelligence segment.
