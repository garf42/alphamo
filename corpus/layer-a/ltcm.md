## Research summary

Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM) is the **canonical systemic-leverage-failure
architecture**: founded 1994 by John Meriwether (former Salomon Brothers head of fixed-
income arbitrage) + Myron Scholes (Nobel laureate 1997) + Robert Merton (Nobel laureate
1997) + Eric Rosenfeld + Larry Hilibrand + ~12 other partners. Initial capital ~$1.25B
from ~80 limited partners + institutional investors [E]. Architecture: quant-arbitrage
strategies (convergence-trades + relative-value + spread-trades + bond-arbitrage + merger-
arbitrage + emerging-markets) + extreme-leverage (peak ~25-30x balance-sheet-leverage +
effectively >100x including derivative-notional-exposure) + counterparty-concentration
(major Wall Street prime-brokerage + repo + derivative-counterparties) [E].

Architecture's 1995-1997 performance: ~20-40% net returns (after 25% performance fee +
2% management fee) demonstrating quant-arbitrage thesis at scale. Peak ~$4.7B equity +
~$129B+ assets + ~$1.25T+ notional-derivative-positions early 1998 [E].

Architecture's critical-failure: 1998 Russian-financial-crisis (August 1998 ruble-
devaluation + GKO-default + foreign-debt-restructuring) triggered global flight-to-
quality + correlated divergence-rather-than-convergence across LTCM's trades. Liquidity
disappeared simultaneously across multiple-asset-class arbitrage-positions; counterparties
called collateral simultaneously; LTCM unable-to-deleverage at scale without crystallizing
massive losses; equity-base eroded from ~$4.7B to ~$600M in weeks [E].

**Fed-orchestrated bailout**: September 23, 1998 — Federal Reserve Bank of New York under
Bill McDonough convened 14 major Wall Street firms (Bankers Trust + Bear Stearns + Chase
+ Credit Suisse First Boston + Deutsche Bank + Goldman Sachs + JP Morgan + Lehman Brothers
+ Merrill Lynch + Morgan Stanley + Paribas + Salomon Smith Barney + Société Générale +
UBS) to inject $3.625B private-sector recapitalization (~$300M+ each from 11 firms +
~$125M each from 3 firms) in exchange for 90% of LTCM equity [E]. Bear Stearns notably
declined to participate; subsequently identified as having limited-counterparty-risk to
LTCM but established precedent that became consequential 2008.

Architecture wound-down through 2000; original limited-partners received partial
recovery; ~16 partners returned ~$0 personally (substantial-collective-net-worth loss
including Nobel laureates Scholes + Merton; Scholes pursued additional ventures including
Platinum Grove Asset Management which collapsed 2008 amid second-major-leverage-event for
Scholes-affiliated architecture) [E].

Architecturally, ltcm is canonical:
- **Systemic-leverage-failure architecture**: extreme-leverage + counterparty-concentration
  + tail-risk-realization producing systemic-implications-requiring-Fed-intervention. 1st
  canonical instance preceding 2008 multi-architecture-failure.
- **PhD-+-Nobel-laureate-quant-arbitrage architectural-template** failed-instance:
  validates that academic-pedigree-+-mathematical-sophistication-+-historical-data-
  modeling does not protect against tail-risk-realization.
- **Pattern #11 (Failed-architecture-as-cultural-cautionary-asset) 8th instance**: "LTCM"
  + Lowenstein When Genius Failed (2000) + Roger Lowenstein book + canonical case-study
  for systemic-risk + leverage-management + tail-risk + correlated-divergence.
