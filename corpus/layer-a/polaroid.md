## Research summary

Polaroid Corporation was the canonical 20th-century instant-photography integrated-
architecture, founded 1937 by Edwin Herbert Land + George W. Wheelwright III in Cambridge MA.
Land's polarizing-filter patents (1934) provided early commercial foundation. Land Camera
Model 95 launched November 1948 introduced instant-photography to consumer market — capture
photograph in 60 seconds without lab processing [E].

The architecture's load-bearing-asymmetry was capability-asymmetry-dominant: integrated
chemistry-optics-manufacturing-+-film-camera-bundle that no competitor matched for ~30
years. Land + Polaroid Research developed in-house: instant-developing chemistry, dye-coupler
films, peel-apart Type 100 series 1963, SX-70 integral-film system 1972 (no peel-apart,
single sheet), Polavision instant-movies 1977 (failure), Sun 660/600/Spectra cameras 1980s,
600 + Spectra integral-film systems [E].

Peak 1978: ~$1.4B revenue + ~21,000 employees + ~80%+ instant-photography market share +
canonical brand-recognition + Land as architect-+-CEO 1937-1980. Kodak attempted instant-
photography entry 1976 → 1986 patent-infringement court ruling for Polaroid → $925M
settlement 1991 from Kodak [E]. Polaroid won the patent war + collected substantial damages,
but the substrate-shift to digital was already accelerating.

The substrate-shift defeat 1996-2008: digital cameras (Casio QV-10 1995 + later) +
camera-phones (Sharp J-SH04 2000 + later) + smartphone-cameras + Instagram/iPhone era 2007-
onward — instant-photography substrate effectively dissolved. Polaroid's response failures:
(a) attempted-but-underinvested digital cameras + Polaroid Digital Imaging 1996-2001, (b)
PolaPro instant-film maintained at declining scale, (c) executive management instability
post-Land-era (Land departed 1982; subsequent CEOs included Mac Booth + Gary DiCamillo +
Jacques Nasser advisory), (d) accounting + pension issues + LBO 1980s legacy debt [E].

October 12, 2001 — Polaroid Corp filed Chapter 11 (first bankruptcy). Sold to Jacques
Nasser-led OEP Imaging 2002 $255M. Operated under successor-Polaroid LLC. December 2008 —
second bankruptcy (Polaroid B.V. / Tom Petters fraud-related parent issue). Sold to PLR IP
Holdings (Hilco + Gordon Brothers) 2009 for $89.5M [E].

2008 — Polaroid announced discontinuation of all instant-film production. 2008 — The
Impossible Project (Florian Kaps + Dr. André Bosman + others) acquired Polaroid's
Enschede Netherlands factory + began reverse-engineering instant-film. 2010 — Impossible
Project began producing new instant film for original Polaroid cameras. 2017 — Impossible
Project acquired Polaroid brand rights + renamed Polaroid Originals. 2020 — renamed back
to Polaroid; operates as Polaroid B.V. under European Investment House ownership today
[E]. Current operations: niche-enthusiast instant-photography + film + cameras + apparel
licensing + cultural-brand-licensing — substantially-smaller scale than Polaroid Corp peak.

Architecturally, polaroid is the **canonical Sub-pattern B substrate-shift case alongside
Kodak film** per watchpoint #1: original architecture (instant-photography integrated-
hardware-+-film-+-chemistry) defunct as mass-medium; niche-persistence via Polaroid B.V.
+ third-party-licensees at ~5-10% of peak revenue scale; cultural-brand persists. Also
contributes to **failed-architecture-as-cultural-cautionary-asset** (Layer D candidate
pattern): Polaroid's failure-to-pivot-to-digital is canonical business-strategy case-
study material.
