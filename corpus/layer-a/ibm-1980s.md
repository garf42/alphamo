## Research summary

IBM's mainframe-era integrated-vertical-business-model architecture (~1956-1985) was the
canonical mid-20th-century computing architecture: combined mainframe hardware (System/360
1964 + System/370 1970 + variants) + software + operating systems + storage + peripherals
+ services + sales + leasing + Federal Systems all under one corporate roof. At its 1985
peak, IBM generated ~$50B revenue + ~$6.6B net income + employed ~400,000+ people + held
~70% of the mainframe market + commanded ~80% of computing-systems profit globally [E].

Three foundational events shaped the architecture's trajectory: (a) **1956 DOJ Consent
Decree** required IBM to sell (not just lease) computers + separate service business + cap
certain practices — first antitrust-preservation event, architecture continued under
restrictions; (b) **1969 DOJ second antitrust case** alleging mainframe-market monopolization;
13 years of litigation; **DOJ dropped case January 1982** during Reagan administration —
Sub-pattern C case-dropped variant; (c) **1981 IBM PC launch** with Intel + Microsoft as
third-party suppliers gave rise to the PC clone industry that would eventually undermine
mainframe-era integrated-architecture from below [E].

The architectural cliff: 1985-1993. IBM's mainframe revenue + profit base eroded as: (a)
client-server computing displaced mainframes for many workloads, (b) PC clones (Compaq +
Dell + others) commoditized PC hardware, (c) Microsoft + Intel captured operating-system +
processor profit pools, (d) Unix + later Windows NT servers displaced mainframe-class
workloads, (e) Sun Microsystems + DEC + HP gained workstation + server share. IBM's 1991-
1993 losses were among the largest in US corporate history: -$2.86B 1991, -$4.97B 1992,
-$8.10B 1993 (cumulative ~$16B in three years). 1993 was the existential year [E].

April 1993 — Louis V. Gerstner Jr. named CEO (former RJR Nabisco + American Express + McKinsey).
First non-IBM-internal CEO + first non-engineer-CEO in IBM history. Gerstner's strategic
turnaround 1993-2002: (a) preserved integrated-IBM (rejected breakup recommendation that
McKinsey + others had proposed), (b) pivoted toward services + integrated-solutions
(IBM Global Services), (c) embraced Internet + open-standards (Java + Linux + middleware),
(d) divested PC business to Lenovo $1.75B 2005 (post-Gerstner under Palmisano), (e)
focused on enterprise software (Lotus 1995 $3.5B + Tivoli 1996 + Rational 2003), (f)
maintained mainframe-as-niche-strategic-position [E].

By 2002 Gerstner departure, IBM had transformed from mainframe-hardware-led to services-+-
software-led architecture; revenue grew $64B → $96B but mix shifted dramatically. Sam
Palmisano CEO 2002-2012, Ginni Rometty 2012-2020, Arvind Krishna 2020-present. Architecture
continued transformation: Red Hat $34B acquisition 2019, Kyndryl IT-services spinoff 2021,
Watson + AI focus + 2025 hybrid-cloud-+-AI positioning [E].

Watchpoint #4 classification: IBM-1980s is in the **Sub-pattern B vs Sub-pattern D zone**.
Arguments for Sub-pattern B (substrate-shift, defunct-with-niche-persistence): mainframe-
era integrated-architecture defunct as architectural-template; IBM still operates but
focused-substantially-narrower (services + software + niche-mainframe + AI/cloud), more
analogous to Kodak film business (defunct as mass-medium-architecture, persists in niche)
than to Berkshire textile-to-capital-allocation. Arguments for Sub-pattern D (operator-
voluntary-transformation): Gerstner deliberately pivoted to services + software + open-
standards by operator-choice rather than substrate forcing; corporate vehicle preserved +
substantially-different-architecture-built using IBM corporate shell; pivot was strategic-
intent rather than forced-substrate-shift.

**My classification: Sub-pattern D (architecture-voluntarily-transformed-by-operator) — 3rd
candidate instance** alongside Berkshire 1965 + Twitter/X 2022. Distinct from Sub-pattern
B (Kodak) in mechanism: substrate didn't disappear (mainframes continued + corporate
computing continued); operator chose to transform architecture in response to anticipated
substrate-shift before forced to. **This is the architecturally-load-bearing instance** —
3rd Sub-pattern D candidate triggers v1.5 schema amendment threshold per user's
watchpoint #4 instruction.
